### Chaymae Loukili

I study Data and Information Science (3rd semester) at TH Köln. What draws me is learning how to connect, analyze and visualize where different data sources meet, then following the emerging questions and hypotheses, to uncover the web of cause and effect, how phenomena are interconnected.

I completed Harvard's CS50 on programming with Python, and I'm currently working through Computational Neuroscience from the University of Washington. I'm learning this on my own, alongside my studies. What pulls me toward research, and later medicine, is wanting to understand the complexity of people and the world. Neuroscience is only one part of that, and I don't know yet where it will lead, but for now this is where my curiosity takes me.

In our Data Integration module, we're looking at [cinema visits in Germany](https://github.com/chaymaeloukili/data-integration-projekt), how streaming has changed them, and whether people are finding their way back to analog, shared experiences.

On the side, mainly to learn, I built [The Thin Line Between Melancholy and Rumination](https://github.com/chaymaeloukili/melancholy-vs-rumination), my first own data project. I explored when melancholic music comforts us and when it pulls us down, using a survey with about 2,400 people. One thing that stayed with me is that beauty runs through everything. An open question for me is what brain data would show, and whether it tells the same story as what people say about themselves.

Something that's always with me is the need to know how humans function and how everything holds together, from society and our health all the way down to physics, since in the end everything is physics, then chemistry, then biology.

*Just a third-semester student trying to find the patterns in herself, what moves her, where the path is heading, and where the gaps still are, so she can build the right skills once she knows what she's really after.*
